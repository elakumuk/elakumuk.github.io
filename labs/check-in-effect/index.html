The Check-In
Effect

When the market gets rough, does seeing losses more often change how you feel—and how much risk you choose?

Prototype only. Historical U.S. market returns for 2020 are used to build the hypothetical portfolio path. Your answers stay in this browser session and are not saved or transmitted. No financial advice.

SETUPRANDOM ASSIGNMENT

Take part in a short decision exercise

You will see a hypothetical investment move through a volatile period. At selected points, you will receive a performance update and choose an allocation. The underlying return series is the U.S. market excess return plus the one-month Treasury bill rate; your chosen risky-asset share is combined with that same bill rate. This is not a test of intelligence or investing skill.

  • One fictional portfolio; no real money
  • Allocation choices use a 0–100% scale
  • About 3 minutes; you can stop at any time

What this prototype is testing

The eventual study will randomize participants across two independent factors: how often performance is shown and how often an allocation can be changed. This demo exercises that flow only; its tiny convenience sample cannot test a hypothesis. Historical data source: Kenneth French Data Library (monthly Fama/French factors; 202608 archive vintage).
