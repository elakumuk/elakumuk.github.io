Charcoal, ballet, and the things that are not on a résumé

Between Memory and Invention

Six charcoal drawings · 2025

I made this series in March 2025 during my third year at Brandeis University in Waltham, Massachusetts. Living through a New England winter, far from the landscapes I grew up with, I found memories of the Aegean and Mediterranean returning through the act of drawing.

I began without predetermined compositions, at times working on three drawings simultaneously and allowing them to develop alongside one another. I had long been drawn to perfection and planning; here, I wanted to release that impulse. Charcoal became central to that process. I worked it directly with my hands, diffusing, disturbing and erasing it, then returned with charcoal pencils to create sharper marks. Its ability to move between atmosphere and precision allowed both control and surrender.

Although I approached the works as abstraction, forms emerged as I worked: oceanic rhythms, vegetation, landscapes, and fragments of places and memories. Having spent much of my childhood around the Aegean Sea, its patterns remain part of my visual memory. In Massachusetts, those impressions surfaced indirectly rather than as literal depictions.

The drawings move between recognition and dissolution. A mark can suggest a landscape or remembered place and then return to abstraction. The resulting works exist somewhere between memory and invention, between what is remembered and what is being made in the present.

Resolution

Drag to pull back

Pull the slider back and the same sheet you just looked at goes from texture to drawing. Nothing on the paper changed — only the resolution you read it at.

That is not a metaphor I reached for afterwards. It is the same failure I spend my working hours guarding against: the H-1B study on the other page returns a clean null at the aggregate and a real pattern one level down, on the same rows. Reading at the wrong resolution is how you miss a finding — and, just as often, how you invent one that was never there.

Teaching

2021 – 2022 · Turkey

I am a certified ballet instructor. For two years I taught ballet to children in Turkey who had no prior access to the arts — kids who had never been inside a studio and had no reason to think one was for them.

I keep it on this page rather than under work experience because it does not belong to my career. But it is the thing that taught me how to explain something difficult to somebody who has no vocabulary for it yet, and how to keep a room of people engaged when I am the one standing at the front. I use both constantly.

What your body reports and what is actually happening are not the same thing. You learn to read the gap — and to distrust your own reading on the days you badly want a particular answer.
