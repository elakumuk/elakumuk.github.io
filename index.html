Can an LLM be your marketing analyst?

Same campaign, three methods, three different answers

It named every assumption it needed to check, and then signed off on the number those assumptions rule out.

+5.53pp Apparent campaign lift
naive treatment minus control, highly significant
≈ 0pp Once you control for who was already engaged
the whole effect was selection bias

I gave a language model the same marketing campaign data I analysed properly, and checked whether it could tell correlation from causation. Users who saw more impressions were already the more engaged users. Control for the covariates and the effect goes to zero. Propensity score matching does not rescue it either — the two groups separate almost perfectly on propensity, so there is no overlap to match on. That failure is itself a result: not every method works on every dataset.

The model cited unconfoundedness, SUTVA and the need for regression controls — and then accepted the 5.53 figure anyway. It knew every concept and could not execute one of them.

My role
Study design · the econometrics · the protocol the model was judged against
Method
Naive difference · OLS with pre-treatment covariates · propensity score matching · overlap diagnostics · prompt-framing sensitivity

The most dangerous way to use one of these models is to ask it to confirm something you already believe. Given a leading prompt it pushed back — and then engaged with the biased framing anyway.

When is the obvious answer wrong?

The aggregate said nothing. The sub-categories did not.

A difference-in-differences around the September 2025 H-1B proclamation that reads as a clean null — until you stop trusting the average.

My role
Data pipeline · grouping rules · the disaggregation that found it
Method
65,360 POI-month rows filtered to 37,524 on a privacy-reliability threshold · postal codes grouped into tech, university and control areas · mix comparison to check the control group was comparable

At the aggregate level there is nothing to report — the contrasts are small and point in inconsistent directions. Disaggregated, a pattern appears in exactly the places where daily life happens, and it appears on the largest samples in the dataset.

The finding only appeared after I stopped trusting the average — and it survives because it sits on the biggest samples, not the biggest percentages.

Between Memory and Invention

Six charcoal drawings · 2025

I made this series in March 2025 during my third year at Brandeis University in Waltham, Massachusetts. Living through a New England winter, far from the landscapes I grew up with, I found memories of the Aegean and Mediterranean returning through the act of drawing.

I began without predetermined compositions, at times working on three drawings simultaneously and allowing them to develop alongside one another. I had long been drawn to perfection and planning; here, I wanted to release that impulse. Charcoal became central to that process. I worked it directly with my hands, diffusing, disturbing and erasing it, then returned with charcoal pencils to create sharper marks. Its ability to move between atmosphere and precision allowed both control and surrender.

Although I approached the works as abstraction, forms emerged as I worked: oceanic rhythms, vegetation, landscapes, and fragments of places and memories. Having spent much of my childhood around the Aegean Sea, its patterns remain part of my visual memory. In Massachusetts, those impressions surfaced indirectly rather than as literal depictions.

The drawings move between recognition and dissolution. A mark can suggest a landscape or remembered place and then return to abstraction. The resulting works exist somewhere between memory and invention, between what is remembered and what is being made in the present.

Resolution

Drag to pull back

Pull the slider back and the same sheet you just looked at goes from texture to drawing. Nothing on the paper changed — only the resolution you read it at.

That is not a metaphor I reached for afterwards. It is the same failure I spend my working hours guarding against: the H-1B study on the other page returns a clean null at the aggregate and a real pattern one level down, on the same rows. Reading at the wrong resolution is how you miss a finding — and, just as often, how you invent one that was never there.
