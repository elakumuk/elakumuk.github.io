Can an LLM be your marketing analyst?

Two AI analysts, two opposite mistakes

One stopped at the naive number. The other controlled away the campaign’s own effect. Getting it right meant knowing which variables the campaign itself caused.

−0.02pp What a formal-looking regression reported
it controlled for impressions and clicks, which the campaign causes
+5.5pp With pre-treatment controls only
OLS, matching and weighting all agree
Situation
A targeted, non-randomized campaign across 5,000 users showed a +5.53 point lift in conversion. I gave two language models the same data and asked whether the campaign caused it.
What I did
Ran the analysis with pre-treatment controls only — OLS, propensity matching and inverse-propensity weighting — and audited each model’s answer against it.
Outcome
The campaign works: every correctly specified estimate lands between +5.5 and +6.0 points. Gemini reported the naive number as causal; the regression Claude Code wrote controlled for the mediators and collapsed it to zero.

The campaign was targeted, not randomized, so the naive lift mixes the campaign with who was chosen for it. The trap is the next step. Impressions and clicks are caused by the campaign; controlling for them measures its effect with its own mechanism held fixed, which is why a regression with four controls reports zero. With baseline covariates only, OLS, matching and weighting agree, and the treated and control groups overlap almost completely on their pre-treatment propensity.

Rigor is specification discipline, not variable count. A regression that controls for a mediator is more dangerous than a naive comparison, because it looks like method.

My role
Study design · the specification · the audit each model was judged against
Method
Naive difference · OLS with pre-treatment covariates · 1:1 propensity matching · inverse-propensity weighting · mediation decomposition

Asked directly, Gemini rated its own confidence in the causal claim 4 out of 10 — and in the plain prompt still reported the naive 5.53 as the effect.
