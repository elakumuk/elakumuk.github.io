Who actually gets hurt on Massachusetts roads?

Massachusetts crash intelligence

Raw counts say the problem is motor vehicles, because that is where the volume is. Severity rates say something else entirely.

11.1×

A pedestrian crash is 11.1 times as likely as a motor-vehicle crash to kill or seriously injure someone.

Situation
Massachusetts publishes every crash, but raw counts point at motor vehicles, because that is where the volume is.
What I did
Built the pipeline from MassDOT’s ArcGIS services into SQLite — 661,375 records — and a killed-or-serious rule applied the same way to every road user.
Outcome
Ranked by severity instead of volume, the picture reverses: pedestrians and cyclists carry the risk. The bicycle killed-or-serious share fell 27.6% even as volume rose.

A dashboard over the state’s open crash record system, pulled live from MassDOT’s ArcGIS feature services, normalised into SQLite, and filtered by date, road user, hour and street. Built on an MVC split so the data layer, the filters and the views stay independently testable. I also assembled a small panel of municipal traffic-board approvals — stop signs, stop-ahead treatments, reflective striping — with their approval dates, so specific intersections can be studied before and after an intervention.

My role
Data pipeline · grouping methodology · dashboard design
Method
ArcGIS REST ingestion · SQLite normalisation · KSI classification rule · MVC split with pytest coverage
