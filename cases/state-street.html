When can you trust a signal?

AI-augmented multi-asset meta-labeling

Most models predict a direction. This one decides whether the direction has earned the right to be traded.

0.81Sharpe

Walk-forward Sharpe of the recommended version, positive in six of six out-of-sample periods.

Situation
A State Street portfolio team’s multi-asset model — seven asset classes, about fifteen years of weekly data — looked strong on the data it was built on.
What I did
Designed the test around six rolling out-of-sample periods with the sample held fixed across every variant, and built the attribution, the two-layer cost model and the benchmark-relative weighting.
Outcome
The recommended version held up in every period. I owned the narrative and the Q&A with the State Street stakeholder.
Primary signaldirectional view Meta-model asks“do we trust this signal here?” Decisionact · size down · ignore

A research pipeline that sits on top of a multi-asset signal book. A primary layer produces directional views; a meta-labeling layer learns when that primary view is trustworthy, so the strategy sizes down instead of trading everything it sees.

My role
Attribution design · walk-forward validation · transaction-cost modelling · benchmark-relative weighting
Method
Index-level returns rather than ETF · technical signals consolidated into one sleeve · benchmark-relative active weights · every stage re-fit out of sample
Stack
Python · meta-labeling · walk-forward CV · factor attribution · LLM signal extraction

Running my own P&L for three years taught me to distrust a backtest that has never paid a transaction cost. That is why the cost model here has two layers.
