Can you actually buy what the model wants to hold?

A token research desk where every number has to prove itself

Twenty tokens profiled for tradable liquidity, value accrual to holders, and how much a portfolio can own before the market stops absorbing it.

17of 20

positions hit their liquidity cap in a $1B portfolio — at that size it becomes a different strategy.

Situation
Crypto screens rank tokens by daily volume. A fund pays the order book, and needs to know whether a protocol’s fees ever reach the people holding its token.
What I did
Walked live Coinbase and Kraken books for $100K–$10M orders, split protocol fees from holder revenue, capped each position at 10% of median daily volume, and put every memo number behind a verifier.
Outcome
AAVE trades $256M a day but has $1.6M within 2% of price on US venues. On a point-in-time universe since 2016, a $100M fund has fit every month since August 2020; a $1B fund never has for long.
Open the live desk Code and methodology
My role
Research questions · liquidity and capacity design · verification rules · reviewing every memo conclusion against governance records
Method
Order-book walks for $100K–$10M orders · Amihud illiquidity · fees vs. token-holder revenue · Ledoit-Wolf covariance · positions capped at 10% of median daily volume · point-in-time universe since 2016, dead coins included

Reported volume and tradable depth tell different stories. The desk measures both, writes a memo per token from a registry of facts, and refuses to publish if any number in a memo does not match its source. A language model may add commentary; a deterministic verifier decides whether it ships.

The verifier guarantees a number matches its source, not that the conclusion is right. When a memo said Aave’s holder cash flow had stopped, I checked governance before believing it — the DAO had paused buybacks.
